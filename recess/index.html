RECESS

Three games you already know the rules to. Getting out needs a reflex, a nerve and a reading — in that order.

Seed —

Built on gama3d · scena3d · anima3d — the hall, the people and the rules are all generated in code.

How to play

Round one — Green Light

  • Hold W to run. Let go to stop.
  • The Watcher takes 0.62 s to come round. Noticing costs you about 0.18 s and stopping costs the rest — so let go while it is still turning, not when it has finished.
  • Anything moving faster than a sway when it is looking is out.

Round two — Panes

  • A and D pick a pane, W steps onto it.
  • One of each pair holds. They are cut from the same sheet and there is no tell — so the only real move is to let someone ahead of you find out first.

Round three — Marbles

  • They say what they are holding. Sometimes it is a lie.
  • H holds the lamp still, B believes them, N calls the bluff.
  • Lying is work, and work dilates a pupil — by about half a millimetre. The light moves it by five and a half. So you can only read them with the lamp held, and you only get three holds.

What it is built on

Nothing in here is a difficulty curve tuned by feel. Each round is built on a measurement somebody published, and the game is what happens when you have to live inside it.

  • Round one — Donders measured simple reaction time in 1868 and got about 180 ms. It is still about 180 ms. Deciding which signal arrived roughly doubles it. Every rival on that field is drawn a skill, and whether they die is whether their reaction plus their braking fits inside the Watcher's turn.
  • Round two — an honest coin. The panes share one material instance, so there is no tint or seam to read the answer off. What varies is who pays for the information.
  • Round three — everyone knows liars look away. They do not: DePaulo et al.'s 2003 meta-analysis found gaze aversion essentially unrelated to deception. What does hold is that lying is effortful, and effort dilates the pupil — Hess & Polt (1964), Kahneman & Beatty (1966) — by roughly half a millimetre. Moon & Spencer (1944) has the light worth five and a half. Eleven to one, which is why the lamp is the mechanic.

Inspired by the survival-game genre; the rounds are folk playground games and every asset here is generated procedurally.
